# Week 02 — Choosing a Direction


## Exploring Ten Ideas

This week, I tried to make my broad interest in embodied role-play more specific. I considered ten possible directions:

| Idea | Initial interaction |
|---|---|
| 1. Mongolian nomadic life | Observe grass, livestock behaviour, weather, and terrain to decide when to move “home.” |
| 2. Living in an unfamiliar language | As language becomes harder to understand, use gestures, people’s actions, objects, and spatial relationships to navigate everyday life. |
| 3. Sensing seasonal time | Without a calendar, interpret temperature, sunlight, plants, animals, and rain to work out the time of year. |
| 4. Tai Chi: seeing “qi” | Turn arm movements into flowing particles or visual trails. |
| 5. Experiencing ageing | Explore everyday tasks through selected changes in vision, hearing, mobility, or memory. |
| 6. Dining across cultures | Discover expectations around utensils, seating, shared food, eating order, and conversation. |
| 7. A Chinese mother’s day | Explore household planning, shopping, price comparisons, bargaining, and everyday decisions about time and money. |
| 8. Chinese white dolphin survival | Take an animal’s perspective to explore feeding, movement, boats, pollution, and habitat loss. |
| 9. Sleep and daily routines | Arrange a character’s day and explore how activities and habits could affect their sleep. |
| 10. Living with persistent alertness | Explore how sustained stress and vigilance could shape the experience of ordinary situations. |



## Mentor Feedback

My mentor encouraged me to stay with the idea I liked most: **the Mongolian nomadic life simulator**.

The suggested next step was to research **simulation and worldbuilding**, including Ian Cheng’s work, and investigate how multiple environmental variables can affect decisions. Cheng’s *Emissaries*, a series of live simulations made with a game engine, is an initial reference to explore. [MoMA exhibition page](https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/3656?locale=en)

Another important suggestion was to connect cultural information directly to game mechanics. Rather than only explaining a practice, I should consider what the player would need to notice and do to understand its context.

My mentor also recommended keeping one continuous reading document: what I read, what inspires me, and what changes my thinking. This would become useful material for writing in November.

## Choosing the Nomadic Life Direction
Grass, animal behaviour, weather, and terrain could provide different kinds of information. I want to investigate situations where these clues do not all suggest the same answer. However, their relationships still need research; I should not assume that one signal always means it is time to move.
