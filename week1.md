# Week 01 — Initial Research and Reflection

## 1. Starting Point

At the beginning of this project, I was interested in 3D mapping and wanted to explore how it could become part of an interactive spatial experience. I was particularly drawn to the possibility of moving beyond a conventional screen and allowing people to encounter information through their bodies and surroundings.

However, I did not yet have a clear cultural subject or a specific interaction to build. My starting point was largely an interest in the technology. I needed to think more carefully about what the experience would help someone understand, and why bodily participation would matter.

## 2. Initial Research Question

> **How do embodied role-play experiences within 3D-mapped spatial game environments shape design learners’ cross-cultural contextual sensitivity?**

My core interest is exploring whether embodied role-playing can help design learners move from abstract cultural knowledge toward a more contextual understanding of other people’s lives.

For example, knowing a cultural fact may be different from making a decision while considering someone’s environment, daily routines, responsibilities, and available resources. I want to explore how spatial immersion and taking on a narrative role might influence the way learners respond to culturally different user needs.

At this stage, “cross-cultural contextual sensitivity” is a working phrase rather than a clearly defined measurement. I am using it to describe a learner’s willingness to notice context, question assumptions, and consider why a decision might make sense within another person’s circumstances.

## 3. Why Embodied Cognition?

I am very interested in 3D mapping, but I do not want the project to rely only on the novelty of the technology. I would like to investigate **embodied cognition** as a possible theoretical foundation.

My initial question is whether actions such as walking, reaching, looking around, or arranging objects could contribute to understanding a cultural situation. I still need to read more before deciding how to connect this theory to a practical design.


## 4. Initial Prototype Direction

My initial intention is to use **TouchDesigner and 3D mapping** to create a small interactive prototype. Rather than building a complete game immediately, I would like to start with one space, one role, and one decision.

A possible structure is:

1. Introduce a person and a simple everyday situation.
2. Let the participant explore relevant objects or spatial clues.
3. Ask them to make a small decision from that role’s perspective.
4. Invite them to explain what they considered and what they still do not know.

The priority would be the experience and its meaning. I have not yet decided whether projection or a VR headset is the most appropriate format, and I have not built or tested this prototype at this stage.

## 5. Early Visual References — Familiar VR Games

The following games provide references for different forms of bodily participation. They are **interaction references, not evidence that VR games improve cross-cultural sensitivity**. These notes are based on public descriptions and imagery, rather than a claim that I have conducted playtests.

### Beat Saber — Movement and Immediate Feedback

![Beat Saber gameplay showing red and blue sabers and approaching blocks](https://i0.wp.com/vrhealth.institute/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/ss_4a2f941e281da9d97aef3566a81282daf7802275.1920x1080.jpg?ssl=1&w=1920)


Beat Saber offers a reference for coordinating physical movement with visible and audible feedback. Its rhythm-based interaction makes bodily action central to play. [Official website](https://beatsaber.com/)

**My question:** Could a small sequence of actions help someone attend to a cultural practice? I would need to avoid reducing a meaningful practice to simply copying gestures or achieving a score.

### Half-Life: Alyx — Acting Within a Situated Role

![Half-Life Alyx first-person gameplay showing a virtual hand and the surrounding environment](https://www.ausgamers.com/gameres/7473/images/695/rat-a-tat-tat.jpg)


Half-Life: Alyx places the player in a named character’s role and makes interaction with surrounding objects an important part of its VR experience. [Official website](https://www.half-life.com/en/alyx)

**My question:** How could handling everyday objects and responding to a situation help a participant understand a role’s responsibilities? For my project, I am more interested in ordinary decisions than combat.

## 6. Mentor Feedback

My mentor encouraged me to move from a broad interest in immersive technology toward more specific research questions and examples.

The main suggestions were:

- Look at VR artworks that communicate cross-cultural information, especially those concerned with empathy and embodiment.
- Identify techniques that help viewers relate to the culture or subject being represented. How is identification encouraged?
- Examine user evaluations: what is assessed, and what counts as success?
- Consider whether a headset or projection onto walls better suits the intended experience.
- Propose **ten specific kinds of cultural knowledge** that the project could explore.
- Find and read **ten relevant articles on embodied cognition**.
- Develop **three ideas for how participants could use their bodies to learn**.
