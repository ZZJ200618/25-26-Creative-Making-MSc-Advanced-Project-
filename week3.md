# Week 03 — Research and Early Game Direction

This week I looked for research and games that could help me shape the Mongolian nomadic life simulator. I am interested in open exploration, but I want the main activity to be noticing how land, weather, grass, and animals affect a herding decision.

## A few useful research notes

Wilson describes cognition as connected to the body’s interaction with its surroundings. This makes me think that players should learn by moving around and observing the land, rather than only reading information about it [1].

Finke’s research on pastoral households in western Mongolia describes seasonal movement as a series of difficult choices. Families respond to changing conditions and livestock needs, and decisions are also shaped by relationships with other households [2]. This makes the idea more interesting to me: moving camp should not be a simple right-or-wrong answer, and a useful game should leave room for different priorities.

A meta-analysis by Ventura and colleagues found some evidence that VR can support perspective-taking, but did not find a clear increase in empathy [3]. I should not treat immersion as proof that players understand another person’s life. I can instead ask what they noticed, what they considered, and why they chose to stay or move.

Never Alone was made with the Iñupiat community and draws on an Iñupiaq story [4]. It reminds me that cultural material should shape the experience, and that people connected to the culture should help check how it is represented. My game will need its own research and cultural guidance; visual inspiration alone is not enough.

## Early gameplay idea

The player explores a grassland with a herding household. They look at the grass, water, weather, terrain, and animal behaviour, then decide whether to stay or move camp. Later, they see how the decision affected the animals and the land. The clues may point in different directions, so the player has to explain what mattered most to them.

This is still a rough idea. I need to research which clues and consequences are accurate before turning them into rules.

## Visual direction

I want the open landscape and freedom of exploration that inspire me in Red Dead Redemption 2, with the environment carrying useful information. Long sightlines, visible landmarks, a winding river, and changing weather could invite players to explore. As they get closer, grass and animal behaviour can give them details to consider.
